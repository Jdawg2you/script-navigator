# Optimum Health — Discovery Call Script
**Standalone. Built 2026-09-24 for Jesse Stamm.** Intended for Script Navigator.

Pulled from three sources and your own recorded calls:
- **Kyle / ManhattanLife, June 2025** — the sales craft. Qualifying open, the ACA essential-benefits pitch, build-chart framing, trial close, BAMFAM.
- **HealthFirst, July 2026** — the discovery structure. Package of Protection, retirement and income-protection seeds, the gap map.
- **Your own calls** — Livingston 9/15, Boo 9/21, Cosgriff 9/23. What you actually say, and what you actually skip.

**★ marks something you are not currently doing.** Each one cost real work on a recent case. Read those before you use this; everything unstarred is already yours or already in a script you use.

---

## 1 · OPEN

> "Hi, {first}? *(pause)* Hi {first} — this is Jesse, the health insurance guy. How are you?
> *(pause)*
>
> It looks like you asked about more affordable private health insurance {when}. Did I catch you at an okay time?"

*Giving them the option is the point. Most agents launch straight in; asking marks you as a professional whose time is worth respecting.*

## 2 · QUALIFY BEFORE YOU PITCH

> "Before I get into who we are and all the boring stuff, I need to see whether I can even help you. **Have you already found a good plan, or are you still looking for something better?**"

*Kyle's question and it's a good one — it separates a live prospect from someone already covered, in one sentence, before you've spent anything.*

## 3 · SET THE TONE

> "Most agents try to sell you a policy. What we do is different — we look at your whole situation and put together what we call a **Package of Protection**. That doesn't necessarily mean more insurance. It means making sure there isn't a financial risk sitting there that could hurt your family if life doesn't go to plan.
>
> Today I'm just learning about you. Then I'll work out what actually makes sense and we'll walk through it together. Fair enough?"

## 4 · WHY THEY'RE SHOPPING

> "So tell me — what made you start looking at your health insurance options?"
>
> *(listen — take real notes, these are the words you'll use back at them in the presentation)*
>
> "Was it more about the cost, the coverage, or something else that made you say *this isn't working*?"
>
> "And when you picture the right plan — what's the one thing it has to do for you?"
>
> *(write this one short, in their words — "keep Dr. Patel", "never get wiped out by a hospital bill". It's the line you read back on the presentation; the story above is context for you.)*

*Whatever they say, they're right. Agree with them. This is the emotional reason they'll buy, and you'll repeat it back on the presentation call.*

### ★ If they're losing coverage — get the date
> "Is that a done deal? **And what's the actual last day of coverage?**"

★ **Never asked on any recorded call.** It was an open item on Boo and on Cosgriff. The exact date sets the effective date, the special-enrollment clock, and whether there's a gap between a base plan starting and an underwritten policy being approved. "End of the year" is not a date.

## 5 · THE DIFFERENTIATOR

> "I work with a lot of small business owners and self-employed people who are frustrated with what healthcare costs. Most of my clients either have nothing, or have something that feels overpriced and confusing. Sound familiar?
>
> Here's the thing. Every marketplace plan — ACA, Obamacare, same thing — has to include ten essential health benefits. **Most people don't need all ten.** If you're not planning on having a baby, why are you paying for maternity, which is one of the most expensive things a plan can cover? So what I do is tailor a private plan to what you actually need, and we stop paying for the rest."

*This is the strongest paragraph in any of the three scripts. It's Kyle's, and you already say a version of it — this is the tightened one.*

## 6 · WHEN DO THEY WANT IT TO START

> "So assuming we can get you qualified, **when would you want coverage to start?**"

*Usually no sooner than 15 days out; most carriers start on the 1st or the 15th. Asking now sets the pace for the whole case and tells you how hard the deadline is.*

## 7 · FAMILY

> "So I can make the right recommendation — tell me about your family. Married, single, children, grandchildren? Anyone financially dependent on you?"

Names and ages of everyone needing coverage.

## 8 · OCCUPATION

> "What kind of work keeps you busy? Business owner, self-employed, 1099, employee, retired?"
> "How long have you been doing that?"

*If they're changing or retiring:* "Will you pick up another job, or is that you retiring for good?"

## 9 · INCOME ★

> "What did you make this year?"
>
> ★ "And what do you expect the **household** to bring in **next year**?"
>
> ★ *(read it back)* "So about ${X} — have I got that right?"

★ **Both numbers, and the read-back.** Next year's figure decides whether they get a subsidy, and the subsidy decides the entire shape of the recommendation. A spoken dollar amount is the least reliable thing on a recording — one call transcribed "$70,000 a year" as "70 million."

### ★ Do the cliff check while they're talking
Tax credits stop dead at 400% of the federal poverty level. **2026: 1 person $62,600 · 2 people $84,600 · 3 people $106,600 · 4 people $128,600 · 5 people $150,600.**

If they're anywhere near their line, say so on this call. On a recent case the gap between $84,000 and $100,000 of household income was **$1,200 a month** — the same plan, $321 versus $1,521. It is a cliff, not a slope.

## 10 · RETIREMENT *(seed only — no selling)*

> "When you think about retirement, what does that look like? Do you feel like you're on track?"
> "What have you got set aside — 401(k), IRA, Roth, annuity, pension? Roughly how much?"

*An old employer 401(k) they've left behind is a rollover conversation for later. Note it, don't chase it.*

## 11 · INCOME PROTECTION

> "If something unexpected kept you from working for six months — how hard would that be financially?"

*This answer sizes the deductible they can actually carry. "I don't have that kind of money set aside" is the sentence that justifies the whole hospital-indemnity conversation later.*

## 12 · EXISTING PROTECTION

> "Have you already put something in place to protect your family if something happened to you?"

*Work-only life insurance is a gap, not coverage — it leaves when the job does. Note it and move on.*

## 13 · CURRENT COVERAGE

> "What do you have now?"
> "What are you paying a month?"
>
> ★ "And is that your share, or the whole premium?"
>
> "What's the deductible? Any copays?"
> "Did you happen to hit that deductible last year?"

★ **The share-versus-whole question.** Boo said $500; the employer was paying $700 more. Livingston said $580 against a real premium near $1,160. The full number is the honest anchor, and without it you understate what they were really getting.

*On the deductible question — acknowledge the sting. Paying a large premium all year and still owing thousands before the plan does anything is a genuine frustration, and naming it builds more trust than any product feature.*

## 14 · QUALIFICATION ★

> "I see you're in {state} — ★ **what's your ZIP code?**"

*Make a little small talk about where they live.* ★ Not the state, the ZIP. Arizona and Florida both price by county, and without it there is no marketplace comparison at all. **Skipped on Cosgriff, and it blocked the entire case.**

> ★ "And your date of birth?" *(everyone, not just the primary)*
>
> "Do you smoke or use any tobacco?"
>
> "Some carriers use what they call build charts — no need to get on a scale, but roughly **how tall are you and what do you weigh?**"

★ **Real numbers, for both people.** "About 6'1", maybe 290" is a BMI of 38 and it's the underwriting gate for the whole case. The build-chart framing is Kyle's and it works — it makes the question routine instead of personal.

## 15 · HOUSEHOLD

> "Just you, or are we looking at anyone else? Spouse, partner, kids?"

★ **The spouse's date of birth is not optional.** A couple's rate is set off the older applicant, so their DOB changes the price of everything — and on one call the recording put the spouse's birthday on the client's record.

## 16 · HEALTH HISTORY

> "Do any of you have health problems, or take any regular prescriptions?"

★ **Get the drug name right — spell it out if you have to, and note which person it's for.** "Alma Sartin" took a follow-up call to resolve to olmesartan. Mixing up whose condition belongs to whom is the single most common real error in this process.

> "Has anyone been hospitalised or had surgery in the past two years?"
>
> "Any serious history — heart attack, stroke, cancer?"

*If yes, get the detail now; underwriting will ask anyway.*
**Heart attack:** when · how many · treatment and outcome
**Cancer:** type · stage · where · treatment · any recurrence
**Stroke:** severity · other conditions · any repeat episodes

## 17 · FAMILY HISTORY

> "And does cancer, heart attack or stroke run in either of your families?"

*Theirs, not just the client's. This is what drives the living-benefits and specified-disease conversation, and it's your addition to the original script.*

## 18 · DOCTORS

> "Is there a doctor you're in love with — someone you'd really want to keep? What's their name, and when did you last see them?"

*Say nothing about whether they're covered.* That gets checked after the call, on **First Health** and on the base plan's own directory — two different networks, both need checking.

## 19 · DENTAL AND VISION

> "And dental and vision — worth me looking at that for you?"

## 20 · FINANCIAL PROTECTION

> "Do you have a mortgage, or debts you'd want cleared if something happened to you?"

Payment · balance · rough value · how long left to run.

## 21 · BUDGET

> "What's the most you'd be comfortable with a month?"

★ **If they give a range, the top of the range is the number — and read it back.** "Six hundred to a thousand" was captured as 600 and the recommendation came out a tier low. Say: *"So up to a thousand, and you'd stretch if it were worth it — right?"*

*Never build to the budget. It decides which tier you recommend they start on, not what you build.*

## 22 · TRIAL CLOSE

> "I think I can show you a few private plans that fit what you actually need, without paying for what you don't. Would you be open to looking at those?"
>
> "And would you be open to seeing whether adding dental, vision, life or a retirement piece could actually *lower* your total monthly cost while giving you more protection?"

*If it's a simple case and they're engaged, you can build and present now. They've already given you the time — why make them wait?*

## 23 · POSITION THE PRESENTATION

> "Based on what you've told me I already have ideas. Rather than drowning you in dozens of plans, I'll compare several companies and narrow it down to what fits your family.
>
> Health is the first priority. But while I'm in there I'll also look for gaps — sometimes that's life insurance, sometimes retirement, sometimes just better pricing on prescriptions. If I see something I'll point it out. If you're already in good shape, I'll tell you that too."

## 24 · BOOK IT

> "It takes about 30 to 45 minutes, because I'm putting companies side by side and answering everything. Looking at my calendar *(pause)* — I've got {Time A} or {Time B}. Which works better?"

★ **"Will {spouse} be able to join?"** Ask every time. Both decision-makers hear the same thing, and you're not left relying on your client to explain insurance second-hand. *Skipped on Livingston.*

## 25 · HOMEWORK AND CONFIRM

> "Before we meet — could you have your insurance card handy, and a list of any prescriptions? It makes the comparison a lot more accurate."
>
> "I'll send a calendar invite and a text. So we're on for {day}, {date} at {time}. I'm looking forward to helping you and your family."

*Then the digital business card by text from your direct number, so it's from you and not the office.*

# ★ The starred items, and what each one actually cost

| ★ | Last skipped | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Exact coverage end date | every call on record | effective date and SEP clock unknown on Boo and Cosgriff |
| ZIP code | Cosgriff | no marketplace comparison possible; had to be chased afterwards |
| Income next year + read-back | Livingston | the number the whole track hangs on, unverified |
| Subsidy cliff check, live | never done | $1,200/month decision surfaced only after the call |
| Share vs whole premium | Boo, Livingston | anchor understated by roughly half |
| Real height and weight, both | Cosgriff | build chart — the underwriting gate — left as a guess |
| Spouse's DOB | Cosgriff | couple rate is set off the older applicant |
| Drug name, spelled, per person | Cosgriff | took a follow-up to resolve |
| Budget — top of range, read back | Boo | recommendation built a tier low |
| Spouse on the presentation call | Livingston | the one person who has to agree wasn't there |

# What I left out, and why

**"What are your biggest frustrations?"** with the premium/deductible/Rx/network checklist — you have never worked through it. Question 4 gets the same answer faster.

**"Do you have disability income protection?"** — never asked on any recorded call. Question 11 opens the same door without adding a step.

**"What do you enjoy most about what you do?"** — never asked. Good rapport in principle, dead weight in practice.

*All three were cut on usage evidence, not judgement. Say the word and they go back in.*
