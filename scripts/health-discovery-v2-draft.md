# HF Discovery Call — v2 DRAFT (hybrid)
**Draft for Jesse to tweak, 2026-09-24.** Not yet the live script.

Built from three sources: the July 2026 HealthFirst script (`hf-discovery-call-script.md`),
the generated panel script (`callscripts/health-discovery.json`, 23 steps / 45 capture
fields), and **what you actually say on the Livingston, Boo and Cosgriff recordings.**

**Three changes from v1, all evidence-based:**

1. **Cut four questions you have never once asked** on a recorded call — the "biggest
   frustrations" checklist, "did you meet your deductible last year," disability income
   protection, and "what do you enjoy most about what you do." They make the script longer
   than the call.
2. **Five items promoted to MUST — marked ▲.** Each one was skipped on at least one real
   call and cost real work afterwards. They are not optional and the panel should not let
   a call close without them.
3. **One item added that has never been asked**: the exact date current coverage ends.

> **Sync warning.** Changing this file does nothing on its own. `callscripts/health-discovery.json`
> drives the live panel and the field capture, and it must be regenerated to match — otherwise
> the panel keeps prompting the old script and capturing the old fields.

## INTRODUCTION
"Hi, {first}? *(pause)* Hi {first}, this is Jesse, the health insurance guy. You requested
some information about more affordable private health insurance options — {when the request
came in}. Did I catch you at an okay time?"

## SET THE TONE
"Before I tell you about the companies we represent, I first need to work out whether we can
actually help you. Most agents try to sell you a policy. What we do is spend a little time
understanding your situation, put together what we call a Package of Protection, then
reconnect and walk you through it so you're clear on how it works for your family. Today is
just me learning about you. Fair enough?"

**Then the framing you actually use — worth keeping, it isn't in v1:**

"We look at everything available to you — the marketplace, private plans, and plans built to
save money by leaving out the parts you're unlikely to use. If someone isn't going to be
having a baby, why pay for maternity, which is one of the most expensive things a plan can
cover? That's what we're doing: finding what you actually need, at the most affordable price."

## WHY THEY'RE SHOPPING
"So tell me — what finally made you decide to look at your health insurance options?"
*(listen)* "What was happening that made you think something needs to change?"

▲ **If they're losing coverage: "Is that a done deal? And what's the exact last day?"**
Not "end of the year" — the date. It sets the effective date, the SEP clock, and whether
there's a gap between the base plan starting and an underwritten policy being approved.
*Never captured on any call so far. It has been an open item on every case.*

## FAMILY DISCOVERY
"So I can make the right recommendation — tell me about your family. Married, single,
children, grandchildren? Anyone financially dependent on you?"
Names and ages of everyone who needs covering.

## OCCUPATION
"What kind of work keeps you busy? Business owner, self-employed, 1099, employee, retired?"
"How long have you been doing that?"
*If they're changing or retiring:* "Will you take another job, or is that you retiring?"

## INCOME  ▲
"What did you make this year?"
▲ **"And what do you expect the household to bring in next year?"**
▲ **Read the number back: "So about $X — is that right?"**

*Why both, and why read it back.* Next year's number decides the subsidy, and the subsidy
decides the whole track. A spoken figure is the single least reliable thing on the recording
— "$70,000 a year" came back from the transcriber as "70 million."

*While they answer, do the cliff check.* Tax credits stop at 400% of the federal poverty
level. **For 2026: 1 person $62,600 · 2 people $84,600 · 3 people $106,600 · 4 people
$128,600 · 5 people $150,600.** If they are anywhere near their line, say so on this call —
on the Cosgriff case the difference between $84,000 and $100,000 was **$1,200 a month**.

## RETIREMENT
"When you think about retirement, what does that look like? Do you feel like you're on track?"
"What do you have set aside — 401(k), IRA, Roth, annuity, pension?" "Roughly how much?"
*No selling. An old employer 401(k) is a rollover conversation for later.*

## INCOME PROTECTION
"If something unexpected kept you from working for six months, how hard would that be
financially?" *(take notes — this is the answer that sizes the deductible they can carry)*

## EXISTING PROTECTION
"Have you already put something in place to protect your family if something happened to you?"
*Work-only life insurance counts as a gap — it leaves with the job.*

## HEALTH COVERAGE
"Let's talk about your health insurance. What do you have now?"
"What are you paying a month?" ▲ **and "is that your share, or the whole premium?"**
*Both Boo and Livingston gave their payroll share. The real premium was roughly double, and
that is the honest anchor.*
"What's the deductible?" "What do you like about it? What would you change?"

## QUALIFICATION  ▲
▲ **"What's your ZIP code?"** — *not the state, the ZIP. Arizona and Florida both price by
county, and without it there is no marketplace comparison at all. Skipped on Cosgriff.*
▲ **Date of birth** — *each person, not just the primary.*
▲ **Height and weight** — *for everyone, and get real numbers. "About 6'1", maybe 290" is
BMI 38 and that is the underwriting question for the whole case.*
Tobacco use.

## HOUSEHOLD
"Is this just for you, or anyone else?" Spouse, partner, children — DOB, height, weight for
each. ▲ **Spouse's DOB is not optional** — the couple rate is set off the older applicant.

## HEALTH HISTORY
"Any ongoing medical conditions?"
"What prescriptions do you take regularly?" ▲ **Get the name right — spell it if you have to.**
*"Alma Sartin" took a follow-up to resolve to olmesartan. Note which person each one is for;
mixing up whose condition is whose is the most common real error.*
"Any surgeries or hospital stays in the last two years?"

## FAMILY HISTORY
"Does cancer, heart attack or stroke run in either of your families?"
*Drives living benefits and the specified-disease conversation.*

## DOCTORS
"Is there a doctor you'd really want to keep?" Name and location.
*Say nothing about whether they're covered — that gets checked afterwards, on First Health
and on the base plan's own directory. They are different networks.*

## DENTAL AND VISION
"And dental and vision — is that something you'd want me to look at?"

## FINANCIAL PROTECTION
"Do you have a mortgage or debts you'd want cleared if something happened to you?"
Payment, balance, roughly what the house is worth, how long left to run.

## BUDGET
"What's the most you'd be comfortable with a month?"
▲ **If they give a range, the top of the range is the number** — and read it back.
*"600 to 1000" was captured as 600.*

## POSITION THE PRESENTATION
"Based on what you've told me I already have ideas. Rather than drown you in dozens of plans,
I'll compare several companies and narrow it to what actually fits."

## INTRODUCE THE PACKAGE OF PROTECTION
"Health is the first priority, but while I'm reviewing I'll look for other gaps — sometimes
life insurance, sometimes retirement, sometimes just better prescription pricing. If I see
something I'll tell you. If you're already in good shape, I'll tell you that too."

## SET THE APPOINTMENT
"It takes about 30 to 45 minutes because I'm comparing companies side by side. I have
{Time A} or {Time B} — which is better?"

## IF THERE'S A SPOUSE  ▲
▲ **"Will {spouse} be on the call?"** *Ask every time. Both decision-makers hear the same
thing, and you're not left explaining insurance second-hand.*

## HOMEWORK
"Before we meet, could you have your insurance card handy and a list of any prescriptions?"

## CONFIRMATION
"I'll send a calendar invite and a text. So we're on for {day}, {date} at {time}."
*Then the digital business card by text, from your direct number.*

## The five ▲ items, and what each one cost

| ▲ | Skipped on | What it cost |
|---|---|---|
| Exact coverage end date | every call | effective date and SEP clock unknown on Boo and Cosgriff |
| ZIP code | Cosgriff | no marketplace comparison at all until it was chased |
| Income next year + read-back | Livingston | the number that decides the whole track, unverified |
| Height/weight, real numbers, both people | Cosgriff (Matt: "about 61, maybe 290") | build chart is the underwriting gate and it's a guess |
| Budget — top of range, read back | Boo ("600 to 1000" → 600) | recommendation built a tier low |
