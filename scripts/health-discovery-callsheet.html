1

Open

"Hi, {first}? (pause) Hi {first} — this is Jesse, the health insurance guy. How are you? (pause)
It looks like you asked about more affordable private health insurance {when}. Did I catch you at an okay time?"
2

Qualify first

"Before I get into who we are and the boring stuff — I need to see if I can even help you. Have you already found a good plan, or are you still looking for something better?"
3

Set the tone

"Most agents try to sell you a policy. We look at your whole situation and put together a Package of Protection. That doesn't mean more insurance — it means making sure there's no financial risk sitting there that could hurt your family. Today I'm just learning about you. Fair enough?"
4

Why they're shopping

"So what made you start looking?"
"Was it more the cost, the coverage, or something else that made you say this isn't working?"
Whatever they say, they're right. Agree. You'll say it back to them on the presentation.
▲ If they're losing coverage: "Is that a done deal — and what's the actual last day?"
The date, not the month. It sets the effective date and the enrollment clock.
5

The differentiator

"I work with a lot of self-employed people frustrated with what healthcare costs. Every marketplace plan — ACA, Obamacare, same thing — has to include ten essential health benefits. Most people don't need all ten. If you're not planning on having a baby, why pay for maternity, one of the most expensive things a plan can cover? So I tailor a private plan to what you actually need, and we stop paying for the rest."
6

Start date

"Assuming we get you qualified — when would you want coverage to start?"
Usually no sooner than 15 days out. Most carriers start the 1st or the 15th.
7

Family

"Tell me about your family. Married, single, children, grandchildren? Anyone financially dependent on you?"
Names and ages of everyone who needs covering.
8

Occupation

"What kind of work keeps you busy? How long have you been doing that?"
If changing or retiring: "Will you pick up another job, or is that you retiring for good?"
9

Income

"What did you make this year?"
"And what do you expect the household to bring in next year?"
▲ Read it back. "So about ${X} — have I got that right?"
Next year's number decides the subsidy, and the subsidy decides everything.
Subsidy stops at 400% FPL — 2026

If they're near their line, say so on this call. It's a cliff, not a slope — one recent case was $1,200/month either side of it.
10

Retirement · seed only

"When you think about retirement, what does that look like? Do you feel on track?"
"What's set aside — 401(k), IRA, Roth, annuity, pension? Roughly how much?"
An old employer 401(k) is a rollover conversation for later. Note it, don't chase it.
11

Income protection

"If something kept you from working for six months — how hard would that be financially?"
This answer sizes the deductible they can actually carry.
12

Existing protection

"Have you put anything in place to protect your family if something happened to you?"
Work-only life insurance is a gap — it leaves when the job does.
13

Current coverage

"What do you have now? What are you paying a month?"
▲ "Is that your share, or the whole premium?"
Two recent clients quoted their payroll share — the real premium was about double.
"What's the deductible? Any copays?"
"Did you happen to hit that deductible last year?"
Acknowledge the sting — paying all year and still owing thousands is a real frustration.
14

Qualification

"I see you're in {state} — what's your ZIP code?" (small talk about where they live)
"And your date of birth?" — everyone, not just them
"Do you smoke or use tobacco?"
"Some carriers use build charts — no need to get on a scale, but roughly how tall are you and what do you weigh?"
▲ ZIP, not state. AZ and FL price by county — without it there's no marketplace comparison at all.
▲ Real height and weight, for both people. "About 6'1", maybe 290" is a BMI of 38 — that's the underwriting gate.
15

Household

"Just you, or anyone else? Spouse, partner, kids?"
▲ Spouse's DOB is not optional — a couple's rate is set off the older applicant.
16

Health history

"Any health problems, or regular prescriptions?"
▲ Spell the drug name. Note whose it is. Mixing up whose condition is whose is the most common real error.
"Anyone hospitalised or had surgery in the past two years?"
"Any serious history — heart attack, stroke, cancer?"
If yes, get it now — underwriting will ask anyway:
  • Heart attack — when · how many · treatment and outcome
  • Cancer — type · stage · where · treatment · recurrence
  • Stroke — severity · other conditions · repeat episodes
17

Family history

"Does cancer, heart attack or stroke run in either of your families?"
Drives the living-benefits and specified-disease conversation.
18

Doctors

"Is there a doctor you're in love with — someone you'd want to keep? Name, and when did you last see them?"
Don't say whether they're covered. Check afterwards — First Health and the base plan's own directory. Two different networks.
19

Dental and vision

"And dental and vision — worth me looking at that for you?"
20

Financial protection

"Do you have a mortgage, or debts you'd want cleared if something happened to you?"
Payment · balance · rough value · years left.
21

Budget

"What's the most you'd be comfortable with a month?"
▲ A range means the top of the range. Read it back: "So up to a thousand, and you'd stretch if it were worth it — right?"
Never build to the budget. It decides which tier you recommend, not what you build.
22

Trial close

"I think I can show you a few private plans that fit what you actually need, without paying for what you don't. Would you be open to looking at those?"

"And would you be open to seeing whether adding dental, vision, life or a retirement piece could actually lower your total monthly cost while giving you more protection?"
Simple case and they're engaged? Build and present now — they've already given you the time.
23

Position the presentation

"Rather than drowning you in dozens of plans, I'll compare several companies and narrow it to what fits. Health is first priority — but while I'm in there I'll look for gaps. If I see something I'll point it out. If you're already in good shape, I'll tell you that too."
24

Book it

"It takes about 30 to 45 minutes because I'm putting companies side by side. Looking at my calendar (pause) — I've got {Time A} or {Time B}. Which works better?"
▲ "Will {spouse} be able to join?" Every time. Both decision-makers hear the same thing.
25

Homework and confirm

"Before we meet — could you have your insurance card handy and a list of any prescriptions?"

"I'll send a calendar invite and a text. So we're on for {day}, {date} at {time}."
Then the digital business card by text, from your direct number.
