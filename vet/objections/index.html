Veteran / FEX Objections Cheat Sheet

Every objection on a veteran or final expense call, in the order you’ll hear them — the first 30 seconds at the top, the money stalls at the bottom. Straight from the VET script.

Opening objections ↓Closing objections ↓
Never argueAcknowledge, then loop back to the anchor with a close-ended question. E.g. "I hear you… (ask a close-ended question)."
Control the callWhoever asks the most closed-ended questions wins. Open-ended questions hand them the wheel.
Conviction > contentTone, volume, and calm certainty carry it. If you only half believe it, they'll hear it.

Part oneOpening objections

The first 30 seconds. They don’t know you yet — they’re reacting, not deciding. Your job here is to stay in the call.

The anchor (loop everything back to this): they sent in a request and listed their beneficiary for life insurance. Your go-to loop: "Most of my clients are looking to cover burial, cremation, or protect a mortgage — which one was it for you?" Then pivot straight into verifying their info.
They say
"What is this for?"
Say this "I was getting back to you about the request you sent in — where you listed [beneficiary] as your beneficiary for life insurance. Most of my clients are looking to cover burial expenses, cremation costs, or protect a mortgage. Which one was it for you?"
Why it works: re-anchor on the request + beneficiary, then a closed choice pulls them into the conversation.
They say
"I already have coverage / it's handled."
Say this "Perfect — that's exactly why I'm calling. I work with over 40 companies, so I can usually save you a good bit of money. Would you be against saving money?"
Why it works: agree, reframe to savings, and the closed-ended question is nearly impossible to say no to.
They say
"I'm not interested."
Say this "I understand — most of my clients say that because they don't think they can qualify, or someone showed them something that was too expensive. Which one was it for you?"
Why it works: you don't fight the "no" — you give them two reasons and a closed choice that reopens the door.
They say
"I can't qualify."
Say this "I work with over 40 different companies to shop around for you — even with serious health conditions, I can often get people approved. Let me see what I can do to help you. It says here your date of birth is ___?"
Why it works: remove the disqualifier, then pivot straight into verifying DOB — that's how you move forward, not just rebut.
They say
"I can't afford it."
Say this "I work with people on fixed income — Social Security, disability — and still get them affordable coverage. Is that the same situation you're in? No worries, I can help you out. It says here your date of birth is ___?"
Why it works: normalize their situation, reassure, and pivot into DOB to keep the call moving.
They say
"I don't remember filling this out."
Say this "No worries — I don't even remember what I had for breakfast. Most of my clients are looking to cover burial, cremation, or protect a mortgage. Which one was it for you?"
Why it works: a little humor disarms them, then you loop right back to the anchor with a closed choice.

Part twoClosing objections

The money stalls. By now they want it — these are the last flinches before they commit. Don’t argue, don’t email, don’t panic.

The re-close (your reset for every stall): handle the objection, then loop straight back with "Do you want me to go higher or lower, or is ___ good for you?" Any amount going to their beneficiary beats nothing — the best policy is the one that's actually in place.
They say
"I want to shop around."
Say this "That makes sense — and I already did that for you. All insurance is state-regulated, so whether you go straight to the company or through a broker, the price is the same. It's based on your health and your age, and I've shopped the entire market. Do you want me to go higher or lower, or is ___ good for you?"
Why it works: reframe "shopping" as already done, anchor on health + age, and re-close with the higher/lower choice.
They say
"I've seen it for ___ (cheaper)."
Say this "All insurance is based off your health and your age — this is the best rate I could find for you, if I can get you approved. Do you want me to go higher or lower, or is ___ good for you?"
Why it works: they're usually just throwing out a number. Don't debate it — anchor on health + age and re-close.
They say
"I want to talk to ___ first."
Say this "That makes sense. But we don't even know yet if we can get you approved — so start with what you know in your heart would always be affordable. Once we're approved, you talk it over with ___ and adjust up or down. The hard part is just seeing if they'll accept you. Do you want me to go higher or lower, or is ___ good for you?"
Why it works: approval-first reframe — get a small yes now, and leave the door open to adjust later.
They say
"That's too high."
Say this "Yeah, ___ is a lot of coverage. But you'd agree any amount going to [beneficiary] would help them out, right? And since we don't even know if we can get you approved yet, let's start with what you know in your heart would always be affordable."
Why it works: shrink it to an affordable number without losing the sale — keep momentum toward submitting.
